**To get your business on Google Maps, create a Google Business Profile, verify that you own it, then complete the category, hours, photos and website link.** Listing is free. From then on, where you appear in local results depends on how relevant, how close and how prominent your business is, and Google is explicit that a higher local ranking cannot be bought.

This guide covers the setup, the rules people break without realising, and how your website supports the profile.

## How do you set up a Google Business Profile?

1. **Go to Google Business Profile** at business.google.com and sign in with the Google account that should own the profile, ideally one belonging to the business rather than to an employee.
2. **Search for your business.** If a profile already exists, claim it rather than creating a duplicate.
3. **Enter your real-world business name**, exactly as it appears on your sign, receipts or packaging.
4. **Choose the primary category** that best describes the business, such as "Coffee shop" or "Guest house", and add secondary categories only where they truly apply.
5. **Add a location or a service area.** Use an address if customers visit you; use a service area if you go to them or work remotely.
6. **Verify ownership.** Methods include phone or text, email, a live video call, a recorded video or a postcard, and which ones you are offered varies by business. Google says verification reviews can take up to 5 business days.
7. **Complete the profile**: opening hours, phone or WhatsApp number, website, photos, products or services and a short description.

## How does Google rank businesses on Maps?

Google names three main factors for local results:

| Factor | What it means | What you can do |
|---|---|---|
| Relevance | How well the profile matches the search | Accurate categories, complete services and description |
| Distance | How far the business is from the searcher, or from the place named in the search | A correct address or service area |
| Prominence | How well known the business is, including reviews and information across the web | Genuine reviews, a website, mentions elsewhere |

Google also states there is no way to request or pay for a better local ranking, and that more positive reviews and ratings can improve it. Be wary of anyone selling a guaranteed position on Maps.

## What if you have no shopfront?

Mobile repair services, home caterers, photographers and businesses that work online are **service-area businesses**. Google's guidelines ask them to remove the address from the profile if customers are not served there, and to list service areas instead: up to 20, with the overall area no more than about 2 hours' drive from the business location.

## Which mistakes cause problems with a profile?

- **Stuffing keywords into the name**, such as "[name] Cheap Best Café in Town" instead of the business name alone. Google's guidelines require the real name.
- **Using an address you do not actually operate from**, such as a virtual office or a relative's house. Google's guidelines do not accept virtual offices.
- **Creating more than one profile for the same location.**
- **Offering incentives for reviews** or buying them. Google Maps policy prohibits paid reviews and incentives such as discounts or free items. Ask real customers, for example with your profile's review link or QR code.
- **Leaving hours unchanged over holidays**, so customers turn up to a closed door.

## How does your website help on Maps?

The profile and the website reinforce each other:

- **Link the profile to the website**, and the website back to the profile (a map or reviews button).
- **Keep the name, contact details and town identical** across the profile, website, social accounts and marketplaces.
- **Write pages that explain your services and the area you serve**, since those are what match searches like "web design in [town]".
- **Add LocalBusiness structured data** so search engines read your name, business type and area unambiguously.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does it cost anything to be on Google Maps?

No. Google says you can add or claim a business at no cost. Costs only come in if you run Google Ads, which are separate from the profile.

### Can I appear on Maps without a shop address?

Yes, as a service-area business. The address is removed from the profile and replaced by up to 20 service areas within about 2 hours' drive of your location.

### Do I need a website to appear on Maps?

No, but it helps a great deal. A website explains your services, prices and area in full and gives the profile's "Website" button somewhere useful to go.
