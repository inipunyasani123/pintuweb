**A free website is enough to test an idea or cover a short-term need, but for a business that wants customers' trust, the one thing worth paying for is the domain.** Free platforms usually give you an address containing their own name, cap the features and can change or end the service at any time. A domain you own keeps your address the same even when the platform or the developer changes.

## What free options are there?

- **Website builders with a free tier**, usually on a subdomain with limited features.
- **Google Sites**, free with a Google account; to use your own address you need a domain you already own.
- **Free blogging platforms**, good for writing, less so for catalogues or bookings.
- **Free link-in-bio tools**, one page of links on the platform's template.

All of these are legitimate. The point is to understand their limits before the address is printed on signs, receipts and packaging.

## What are the limits nobody mentions?

| | Free website | Paid website on your own domain |
|---|---|---|
| Address | A subdomain carrying the platform's name | yourbusiness.com or a local domain |
| Look | Often shows the platform's branding or ads | Entirely your brand |
| Features | Whatever the free tier allows | What you need: catalogue, booking, payments |
| Moving away | Often hard or impossible to export | Code and domain move with you |
| Continuity | Depends on the platform's decisions | As long as you renew the domain |
| Business email | Usually not available | Possible on your own domain |

## What did the business.site shutdown teach small businesses?

Google used to offer free websites built from a Google Business Profile, on addresses ending in business.site. Google turned them off in March 2024; visitors were redirected to the business's profile until 10 June 2024, after which the addresses stopped working. Businesses that had printed a business.site address on signs or cards had to replace it.

The Business Profile itself still exists and still matters for Google Maps; only the website feature went. The lesson: use free services as tools, but make sure your main address is a domain you control. How to connect a profile to your website is covered in our [Google Business Profile guide](/en/articles/google-business-profile-guide).

## Why does your own domain matter, even for a simple site?

- **The address survives change.** Move platform or developer and the domain simply points to the new site.
- **It is easier to remember and trust.** A short address with your business name looks more credible on a receipt.

## When is free good enough?

- You are testing a business idea and are not sure it will continue.
- The page is for a one-off event, a community group or personal use.
- Nearly all your customers find you on a marketplace or social media, and the site is an extra.

## When is it time to pay?

- Your web address is going on signs, receipts, packaging or ads.
- You want to be found on Google for your business name and services in your town.
- You need a catalogue, orders, bookings or payments that the free tier does not offer.
- You are paying for ads; a fast, clear landing page makes that spend work harder.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can a free website rank on Google?

It can be indexed, but the address carries the platform's name and you control few of the technical settings. Local businesses should also set up a Google Business Profile.

### Can I start free and move to a paid website later?

Yes, though content often has to be rebuilt because not every free platform allows export. If the free address is already widely shared, set up a redirect to the new domain if the platform allows it.

### Is a domain on its own enough?

A domain without a website shows nothing. At minimum, point it to one page with your profile, contact details and location, such as a landing page or link-in-bio page.
