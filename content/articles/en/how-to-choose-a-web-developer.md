**A good web developer can be recognised by their answers to simple questions: who owns the website when it is finished, what the following years cost, and what happens if something goes wrong after launch.** The twelve questions below help you compare quotes fairly before paying a deposit.

We include PintuWeb's answer to each one, so you have a concrete point of comparison.

## Ownership

**1. Whose name will the domain be registered in?**
It should be yours or your business's. *PintuWeb: once paid in full, the domain is entirely yours, together with its access.*

**2. Are the source code and hosting access handed over?**
Without the source code you cannot move to another provider. *PintuWeb: once paid in full, the source code, admin access, hosting and domain are handed over in full.*

## Cost

**3. What exactly is included in the price?**
Ask for the breakdown: design, number of pages, features, domain, hosting, SSL, basic SEO. *PintuWeb: every package is itemised on the [Pricing](/en/pricing) page.*

**4. What will year two and beyond cost?**
This is what most often surprises business owners. *PintuWeb: domain & hosting renewal is around {{renewal}} per year, stated up front.*

**5. How is payment structured?**
Staged payment protects both sides. *PintuWeb: a 50% deposit, and the balance once the website is finished and you approve it.*

## Process

**6. How many revisions are included, and what counts as one?**
*PintuWeb: set by the package (for example, 2 design revisions in the Small Business Standard package); small changes are free during maintenance.*

**7. How long will it take, counted from when?**
*PintuWeb: a landing page in {{duration:landing-page}}, a small business website in {{duration:standar-umkm}}, counted from receipt of the deposit and complete materials.*

**8. Can I see progress along the way?**
*PintuWeb: you can open the preview yourself, and progress updates come over WhatsApp.*

## Quality

**9. Can I open examples that are already live?**
Live examples are more honest than mock-up images. *PintuWeb: {{demos}} demos are open in our [demo gallery](/en/demos).*

**10. How fast are their websites?**
Do not just take their word for it. Run one of their examples through [PageSpeed Insights](https://pagespeed.web.dev/) and see for yourself. *PintuWeb: a PageSpeed score target of {{perf}} — a target, not an average claim.*

**11. Is basic SEO included?**
At minimum: page titles and descriptions, a sitemap, HTTPS, a heading structure and a mobile-friendly layout. *PintuWeb: included in every website package, plus structured data.*

## After launch

**12. What does the guarantee cover, and how does support work?**
Get it in writing: how many days, what is covered and how to report issues. *PintuWeb: a {{guarantee}}-day bug-fix guarantee, then maintenance as included in the package.*

## Red flags to watch for

| Red flag | Why it is risky |
|---|---|
| No mention of year-two costs | A cheap start can be followed by expensive renewals |
| Domain registered in the provider's name | Hard to move or take over the website |
| No examples you can open | The end result is hard to picture and judge |
| Full payment requested up front | You lose leverage if the result is not right |
| A promise of “guaranteed page one on Google” | Nobody can guarantee organic rankings |

## Frequently asked questions

### Freelancer or agency?

Both can be good. What matters is not the size of the team but the answers to the twelve questions above and work you can actually open.

### Is a cheap service always bad?

Not always. A low price is fair for a simple website. Problems arise when a low price comes with a domain in the provider's name, no guarantee or unclear year-two costs.

### What should be written in the quote?

Scope (pages and features), price and payment schedule, timeline, number of revisions, guarantee, renewal costs and cancellation terms.
