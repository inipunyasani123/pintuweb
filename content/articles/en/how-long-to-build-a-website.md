## What are the stages of a web project?

1. **Consultation.** You describe the business, the goal of the site and examples you like. A clear goal shortens every later step: in a Nielsen Norman Group survey of UX practitioners, 83% of projects that began with a discovery phase were reported successful, against 52% without one.
2. **Quote and deposit.** Package, features, price and schedule go in writing. Work starts after a 50% deposit.
3. **Structure and initial design.** The page structure and visual direction are approved before building, so big changes do not land at the end.
4. **Build.** Pages are built, content goes in, forms and WhatsApp buttons are wired up and basic SEO is set.
5. **Testing and revisions.** You open a preview on your own phone and send the revisions your package includes.
6. **Launch.** The domain is connected and the site goes live; after the final payment every login is handed over.

## What usually causes delays?

- **Content is not ready.** Business description, services, prices and photos are the raw material; without them a design can only hold placeholder text.
- **Too many decision-makers.** Feedback arriving from several people at different times turns one revision round into three.
- **Features added midway.** Small additions usually fit in; large ones such as online payments need their own quote and schedule.
- **Nobody knows who holds the old domain.** If the business already owns a domain, find out where it is registered and who has the login.
- **Slow feedback.** Every day spent waiting for an answer adds a day to the schedule.

## How can you speed it up?

- **Write a short brief**: who your customers are, what you want them to do on the site, and three websites you like and why.
- **Gather content in one place**: a shared folder with the logo, photos, service list and prices.
- **Name one point of contact** who collects feedback from the team and sends it as one list.
- **Decide on must-haves versus nice-to-haves** before the quote, so the first version launches on time and extras follow.

If something is missing, we can draft copy from a short brief or use licensed stock photos for now.

## After launch, when will the site appear on Google?

Not immediately. Google says crawling can take anywhere from a few days to a few weeks. Submitting a sitemap in Search Console helps Google find your pages, but rankings still build gradually through useful content. Our [new website SEO checklist](/en/articles/new-website-seo-checklist) lists the steps; local businesses should also set up a [Google Business Profile](/en/articles/google-business-profile-guide).

## Frequently asked questions

### Can a website be finished in one day?

For small projects, yes: a digital invitation takes {{duration:undangan-digital}} and a simple landing page {{duration:landing-page}}, provided every piece of content is ready at the start.

### Does the clock start when I pay the deposit?

It starts when the deposit is paid and the content is complete. If content arrives later, the schedule moves with it.

### Why does an online store take longer than a regular website?

There are more parts to test: catalogue, basket, payments, shipping and the order dashboard. The Full Online Store takes {{duration:toko-online-full}}.

### Do revisions add time?

The revisions included in your package are already in the estimate. Extra rounds, or a change of design direction after approval, can extend the schedule.
